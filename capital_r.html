capital R

🌱 tags: inkhaven updated: 2026-11-25 Wed 22:18, 2026-11-25 Wed 21:56

I fell in love, one time, with a man on the metro. He was silvering, and in a rust collored khadi shirt, and we couldn't keep ourselves from making eye contact, delicately alternating one-camera shots stitched together like episodic memory does. You know those times? —when your clock goes a little funky, accidentally perfect ordering, no way to sum up the parts, so you keep all the parts. It's a beautiful thing, love. We forget this because of the (aforementioned) complicated consequences.

I feel it with a potato in my hands, too. In the delicate, rough skin, leaking starch onto my wet fingers. In the way a knife will slit itself into the gaps between cells, or miss and split them open when the knife is dull or my hands too clumsy. Once my brother told me that there's a kind of yam where when you run it through a grater, something the texture of a marshmallow comes out. When you slice into the skin of a dough, it seizes away from the cut, in anticipation of the bloom it's going to feel in the oven as its insides turn to steam.

If the brain is an inference machine, it must infer from time to time that we are alive. Like everything in the world that curls and unfurls and changes, it must notice the change when we change.

Once upon a time, love was to me what a petal is; something I was afraid of bruising by touching, something that I wanted very much to touch. Once upon a time it was something that happened to me in sticky summer heat, and the sounds of the million little things in my body that go tick and click and squelch and thump. Once upon a time love was to me the ecstasy of the air in an orchestra-hall, shivering and trembling and transmitting secret consequences to the wood of the walls and the drums of the ears and the bodies of the mice in the floorboards.

I like when people say things without saying things. I like dancing in the late night, speaking quietly. I like the Gnossienne, the late stage of faded stories when the minor key notes of remainders tell you that even after all this time, something is still happening here. I like the pain and uncertainty, the places where I still seize up, can't help my faults, am instead trying to be something more like myself even though I still don't know what I am yet. I like the soft sand at the shore of this bitch of a world, and the sense that it's so incomprehensibly vast that you could die at any moment and the only thing that ever mattered about you was that moment when you were twelve years old on the highest branch of a tree that you dared to climb to while a storm was swaying it, swaying and laughing madly along.

I like the sense that there is something else out there, something that doesn't know how you feel about it yet, something that doesn't yet know how it feels about you.

Shadows of collarbones, unanswered texts, afternoon sunlight. The cold, even though I hate the cold, when it smells like rain I still forgive the cold. A corner shop selling paneer, white like nothing else I've ever seen is white. Feet snuck under me on my couch. The taste of white tea.

The sense, that I try to forget because it's so incessant, that more is possible.

I could go on.
